# Transition pro tips for the tasteful transfem

## HRT
HRT will give you boobs, slightly nicer skin, better body smell, will make your body hair grow slower and softer, and will redistribute your fat to be more female-typical, as well as signaling to other trans people that you're serious about being one of us. You're going to have a lower libido (don't worry, you can still get horny) and possibly will become infertile, maybe even permanently, so consider freezing your sperm if you want to have biological children in the future.

If you're not on HRT, get on it. It's very simple and safe, and reasonably cheap. Don't bother with an endocrinologist, all they do is give you basic advice that you can find on any DIY HRT website, and maybe they write you a prescription for meds that are super easy to get anyway. Consistency is key.

I started with pills (which are over-the-counter in russia and kazakhstan), they have slightly worse potential side effects compared to injections, and are also a bit pricier than injections. If it's easy to get where you are, then it's a solid choice. You can do your research on patches or gels if you want that, I know some girls who do it, and it works for them. When doing research, a lot of the time websites will say that a medication requires a prescription even though a pharmacy will sell it to you without one.

These days, I inject estrogen that some trans girl in a foreign country brews in a cauldron. It's actually pretty easy and cheap, once you know what you're doing. A vial is like $60 and will last you a year or longer, you can inject once every 10 days or so, injection supplies are easy to get, and you can inject into both fat and muscle. It's really hard to fuck up an injection, you just stab yourself with a needle, the hardest part is doing it for the first time. Bioidentical estrogen is a pretty simple molecule, so what you're getting is almost certainly high-quality.

## Makeup
Do makeup, but expect to look like shit the first few times you do it. With practice, it will make you significantly prettier.

### Mascara
Mascara is the stuff you put on your eyelashes to make them a little darker and more noticeable, it's super easy to use and the effect is hard to fuck up - you will basically always look better. Just put it on your eyelashes, look up tutorials because there's some advanced pro tip.

### Concealer
I have large eyebags and concealer under my eyes really helps me brighten my face. Get the store clerk to help you pick out a shade that matches your skin, or it'll look off. You can make it last longer without caking by applying powder on top of it.

### Blush
Danger zone! Blush can make you look good or it can make you look bad, depending on your technique and the color you choose. Start subtle and experiment. 

### Foundation
Foundation is a goo that you put on your whole face to make it look like you're made out of injection-molded plastic of a uniform color and texture. I think it doesn't look very good, so I don't do it. But it can help if you have pimples on your face or something.

### Eyeliner
Eyeliner is really hard to apply, takes a long time, and if you do it wrong it makes you look odd. Nevertheless, it can really go a long way in making you hot. Experiment with it, but don't worry if you can't make it work, I don't ever wear eyeliner because I can't apply it for shit.

### Lipstick
A lipstick that doesn't look too unnatural can be nice. I don't wear it because I can't help but smudge it. There's also lip gloss for a more subtle effect.

### Extra thoughts on makeup:
- Experimentation and practice are key. I learned makeup by doing it every day for school, you should do it often and start with a subtle look that you slowly evolve. After some practice it takes 10 minutes to apply and makes you look significantly better. 
- In photos, your makeup will look less pronounced than in real life. So if you're doing makeup just for pictures, put on more.
- If you've got the money, more expensive stuff is noticeably better, but beware that almost all makeup is wildly overpriced, so you might have to shop around. 

## Manners
Smile in pictures, act like a girl, don't worry too much about having a "feminine walk" or stuff like that. There's a fine line between brainworms and reasonable gender transition. Tucking is a meme, don't do it. Take yourself seriously - use female pronouns and your new name when you introduce yourself to people. Be gracious when normies aren't great at intuitively seeing you as a woman - it's hard for people to switch mental gears, even when they have the best intentions. Often, a person's first impression of you will determine what gender they see you as.

## Body hair
My face, armpits, and balls are fine with a razor blade, but trying to use a razor on my legs or arms gives me pretty bad skin irritation, so I use a cheap trimmer for the rest of my body hair. People say that the Philips Oneblade is amazing. You don't need to go for a mirror finish, because that's a lot of effort. If you get a lot of ingrown hairs or skin irritation, it can be very helpful to exfoliate and soak your skin before shaving it.

If your facial hair is still visible after you shave, you can get laser or electrolysis. 

## Skincare
The sun is pretty much Hitler when it comes to skin quality. Don't let it shine on your skin for too long, and when you're going to be out in the sun, apply a high-SPF sunscreen generously. Smoking also negatively impacts skin. Moisturizing is a good idea. There's sometimes interventions for specific skin issues. If you've got wrinkles, botox is not as much of a bad idea as you might think.

Here's a [great guide](https://slop.sus.cat/health/skin-research/webapp/index.html) by my acquaintance [meowtase](https://x.com/cutesuscat) on all things skincare.

## Voice training
Your voice is the one big thing that won't be affected by HRT, you need to improve it yourself. The basic idea is that you need to make your *pitch* only a little higher (to avoid mickey mouse fagvoice), raise your resonant frequency by raising your larynx, and experiment with cadence. Once you find your voice, you need to really get into the habit of using it while simultaneously thinking about what you're saying, and then you will sound nice. 

The classic youtube series ["The Art of Voice Feminization"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BfCS01MkbIY&list=PLYJkVI7LLpknvBww07jnsxbz-_Lkynsry) by TransVoiceLessons is good, but she hasn't been able to organize it into a coherent curriculum, so you're just going to have to watch a bunch of the main tutorials and piece it together yourself. I definitely recommend understanding how voice feminization works rather than trying to invent it from first principles.

## Hair
Wash your hair with shampoo and conditioner, grow your hair out to be as long as possible before it becomes a big inconvenience, brush it, and figure out a hairstyle that works for you. When your hair is longer it can be inconvenient to wash it every time you shower, so you might want to decouple washing your hair and washing your body, that's what cis women do.

## Clothes
Experiment, dress well, go for things that are slightly more feminine and don't expect to instantly look like a woman. As with all things, you need to slowly figure out a style that works for you, and at first you might be uncomfortable. 

Consider wearing a bra - it'll make your breasts look bigger (just don't overdo it). Generally, you have to get comfortable with doing things cis women do, because you're a woman now. 

Consider getting a purse.

## Weight
Lose weight until you're somewhere on the lower side of a healthy BMI. For me, eating less and eating a bit healthier worked. Don't try to burn calories with exercise, that's just unnecessary torture that barely works. Don't try to lose weight too quickly or your body will go into "oh shit it's lean season, as soon as I can eat food again I need to convert it all into fat so we don't die" mode. No one knows how diets work, but the basic idea is that you should measure your weight often and log it in a spreadsheet and do what seems to work.

## Culture
You will find your people, among the various subcultures under the broader genderqueer umbrella. There's the gender-assimilationists, the transhuman non-assimilationists (of which I am one), the femboys, the drag queens, the t4t lesbians, and more. Stay away from the 4chan people, they are a black hole of despair and misery. I'd recommend a healthy blend of being normal, striving for beauty and virtue, being tolerant of others while not losing touch with normal society.

You will likely have an easier time finding people to date and be friends with among your fellow trans girls, many of them will share your interests and aspirations, and for now the culture leans very young and unfortunately left-wing. 

Many trans girls have brainworms, try to spare yourself that fate. In particular you shouldn't aspire to have gender dysphoria, and certainly don't think that not "passing" means you have to be suicidal. Don't be insufferable about your pronouns, getting "misgendered" is rarely indicative of someone trying to offend you. Don't whine about being ugly - it's a self-fulfilling prophecy, and not being satisfied with your looks is a problem that most women, cis and trans alike, have to face and overcome.

In queer culture, you will stay an outsider until you visibly commit to gender transition. Some people will be nice and take you seriously, but until you're out of the closet and actually trying to be a girl, you're on probation.

Don't make being transgender your whole personality.

## Miscellaneous 
- Get your ears pierced, it's simple, cheap, and after a month you just permanently have an extra slot for pretty things to wear
- Every woman has that one thing they don't like about their appearance. Sometimes you can work around it, sometimes you have to make peace with it
- I would advise against changing your legal name or passport gender marker. It doesn't impact your day-to-day, it's cumbersome to do, and the end result is the government knowing that you're a legible minority that can be oppressed 

## The meta-skill of gender transition
The main thing I want to emphasize is that you can do certain things that will make you look more feminine, better, and hotter. You can look different, have people treat you differently, and even make them think you've got XX chromosomes, if that's what you're into. You need to keep putting in the effort, learning, experimenting, and never settling for mediocrity. Trans people are awesome, we've got some of the best culture on earth, lots of incredibly talented people, and a place for just about anyone who doesn't like their previous gender. I hope you enjoy your stay.
