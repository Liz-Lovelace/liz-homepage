# What it's like living in kazakhstan. 
I was born in Russia, and i grew up on the outskirts of Moscow (population: 13 million). When I was 18 years old, my country declared war on Ukraine, and since i had "male" in my passport, i was at risk of being drafted for the war. Dying in a ditch with an ak-47 in my hands is not where i wanted my life to go, and i got a great remote programming job, so i took my $4000 (in a little box of $100 dollar bills) and took a plane to the country of Kazakhstan. I've now lived here for around 4 years, and this essay is about what life is like here, compared to russia and thailand, in which i've also lived, and compared to the US and europe, about which i hear a lot from my friends

## Kazakhstan is easy mode
One of the nicest things about kazakhstan is the cost of living. I rent a room in a 3-bedroom apartment with my best friend and his girlfriend. It's in the center of town and has all the amenities I want (more on soviet housing later), and the rent is $300/mo in 2026 money. I spend around $1000/mo, and my lifestyle is pretty lavish: i go out to eat, buy electronics and good food, i take the taxi instead of public transport. Visa stuff is easy, and the banks will open a bank account for you if you just give them your passport, that can send and receive international wire transfers. You can buy and sell cryptocurrency, rent out an apartment without involving government or city authority, and generally live your life without the omnipresent fear that you're breaking some obscure rule that will land you in prison. The cops/regulators rarely extort bribes, and in big cities there's not much criminal activity. You can walk around at 4am and feel safe. The homeless people aren't crazy. War isn't on the horizon, despite having china and russia as neighbors. The culture is accomodating to foreigners and weirdos.
